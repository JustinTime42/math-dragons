# Win the parent's swipe, then their signup

*Research synthesis, dated 2026-10-19. Confidence labels from the underlying notes are preserved: platform sources are marked official, official-via-secondary, third-party data (3P-data), or third-party opinion (3P); math evidence is marked HIGH, MODERATE, or LOW.*

As of October 2026, the faceless short-form playbook that fits Math Dragons is narrow and clear: make **visibly authored, adult-addressed, 11-30 second loopable videos** with original narration, real gameplay and on-model dragon art, posted 3-5 times a week for at least 90 days, and judge them on engaged views, viewed-vs-swiped and shares rather than raw views. Both YouTube Shorts and Facebook Reels test every video on a small seed audience and expand reach based on whether people stop, watch to the end, rewatch and send it, and both platforms have spent 2025-2026 actively cutting reach for templated or re-uploaded content (Meta since July 2025, YouTube with an October 1, 2026 originality update). That makes the channel's proprietary assets (game art, gameplay, a dad-built origin story, verified math) its main competitive advantage, and makes generic AI-voice-over-stock "slop" its main failure mode. The single largest compliance risk is YouTube classifying the channel as **Made for Kids**, which would disable comments, end screens and notifications and route videos to children; the defense is genuine adult direction, not a checkbox. The second is overclaiming: the research supports "short, regular, low-pressure practice that adapts," not "proven to raise scores." The funnel is structurally multi-hop, because ordinary Shorts still carry no clickable external link and Facebook caption links are paywalled, so every video should push to one short, typeable landing page that routes parents to TestFlight or a Google Group-gated Play closed test and sets tester expectations up front. A code-first pipeline (LLM script with arithmetic checks, ElevenLabs voice, Manim for all on-screen math, Remotion assembly) runs roughly $1-5 per video once templated.

## Both feeds reward the stop, the watch and the send

### Seed-audience testing decides each video on its own merits

YouTube's Shorts system shows each video to "a set of people that may enjoy your video" and then grows or tapers reach based on their response; early views for a new creator are "exploratory," and YouTube publishes no target for its swipe metric (official, 2023, still the most-cited statement) ([OutlierKit transcript of Todd Sherman](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). The closest official hook metric in Studio is **"How many chose to view"**, the percentage of times viewers watched rather than swiped away ([OutlierKit quoting YouTube Help](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). The commonly quoted "70-90% viewed" benchmark is third-party folklore, not a YouTube target ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)).

Raw view counts have become unreliable twice over. Since **March 31, 2025**, a Shorts view counts on every start or replay with no minimum watch time, while the older metric survives as "engaged views," which still drives YPP eligibility and revenue (official) ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)). Effective **August 24, 2026**, YouTube extended start-counting to all formats (official, via secondary coverage) ([Forbes, 2026-08-17](https://www.forbes.com/sites/gabrielalinzainescu/2026/08/17/youtube-changes-how-it-counts-views-handing-marketers-two-numbers-instead-of-one/); [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/)), and Agentio estimated public views would jump about 30% ([Agentio](https://www.agentio.com/blog/youtube-view-vs-engaged-view)). Any benchmark from before 2025 that uses "views" is therefore not comparable. On **October 1, 2026**, a YouTube Help community post said the company would "further prioritize original content and reduce the reach of content re-uploaded," targeting channels that mainly aggregate others' videos and treating minor template edits as non-transformative; no start date or dashboard indicator was given (official, via secondary) ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-shorts-promoting-original-content-reposts-3718668/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/re-uploaded-shorts-lose-reach-as-youtube-favours-original-clips/)). Shorts can run up to 3 minutes for square or vertical uploads since October 15, 2024 (official) ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). A May 2025 YouTube post reportedly said Shorts and long-form use separate recommendation systems with no evidence Shorts hurt long-form (official, via secondary; original not opened) ([CreatiCalc](https://creaticalc.com/blog/do-youtube-shorts-hurt-long-form)). Unverified 2026 changelog items include removal of the Shorts dislike button (June 25), custom Shorts thumbnails (July) and a **Shorts series feature with seasons and episodes** announced September 23, 2026 (3P) ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)).

Meta's own **Facebook Reels system card (updated June 19, 2026)** is the best primary source on either platform. It lists the predictions the ranker makes: how long you will watch, watch versus skip, watch to completion, likes, whether you keep watching reels after opening one, whether you start a session or leave, and negative feedback such as hide or "see less." Its most-cited inputs include how many times others watched the reel to completion, how long others typically watched it, the reel's length, and the author's history (official) ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/explaining-ranking/fb-reels/)). Since June 2025, all Facebook video is Reels with no length cap (official, via press) ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/facebook-videos-are-all-just-going-to-be-reels-now-185807805.html)). Meta claims that views and watch time for original Reels **roughly doubled in H2 2025 versus H2 2024** after it deprioritized re-uploads, stitch compilations and minor edits (borders, captions, speed changes); this is self-reported (official, via press, March 2026) ([TechBuzz](https://techbuzz.ai/articles/meta-doubles-reels-views-with-new-creator-protection-tools); [Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/meta-cracks-down-on-spam-content-copycats-and-impersonators-on-facebook-reels/)). Instagram head Adam Mosseri named watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach** as Instagram's top three signals, with sends "slightly more important for unconnected content" (official, Instagram only, January 2025) ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-2025/738034/)); treat that as a strong hint for Facebook, not a confirmed rule.

### Short, loopable and muted-proof wins reach

The best large dataset, Quso.ai's 108,138 matured Shorts, finds median views peak at **11-20 seconds** (median 901 views) and fall off past 30 seconds (3P-data) ([Quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/youtube-shorts-length)). Nate Black's 35-billion-view analysis via vidIQ found clusters near 13 seconds and near 60 seconds (3P-data) ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). On retention, Quso's 508K-post sample puts the median average view-through near **45%** and the top 10% near **91%** (3P-data) ([Quso.ai](https://quso.ai/research/video-retention-benchmarks)); Shorts above 1M views average about 76% retention ([Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/how-to-analyze-youtube-shorts-performance)), and looping Shorts can exceed 100% APV because replays add watch time ([Shortimize](https://www.shortimize.com/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate)). Specific "3-second hold" thresholds circulating online name no study and should be ignored ([PostEverywhere](https://posteverywhere.ai/social-media-terms/hook-rate)).

The implication is a length ladder by goal. Discovery content (trivia, "can you solve this," gameplay moments) belongs at 11-30 seconds with one idea and a loop; trust content (parent tips, math-anxiety explainers) at 45-90 seconds; math-history stories at up to 3 minutes once an audience exists. Loops work best when the final line runs into the opening line or the answer reveal lands in the final second, so rewatching to check feels natural; end cards work against both YouTube's replay counting and Facebook's completion signal. Captions are mandatory. The famous "85% of Facebook video watched muted" figure is a 2016 publisher stat ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/silent-world-facebook-video/)), but a 2019 survey of 5,616 US adults found 69% watch with sound off in public and 80% are more likely to finish a captioned video (3P-data) ([Beey.io](https://www.beey.io/en/captions-increase-viewership-accessibility-and-reach-23486/)), and parents frequently watch near sleeping kids. Original audio (own voiceover, the game's soundtrack and SFX) also supports originality classification, since Meta says adding captions alone to someone else's video does not make it original ([Campaign ME](https://campaignme.com/meta-cracks-down-on-spam-content-copycats-and-impersonators-on-facebook-reels/)). No sourced data exists on cut frequency; practitioner consensus is a visual change every 1-3 seconds and a re-hook every ~10 seconds on 30-60 second pieces ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-facebook-reels-length-format-retention)).

Hooks must stack visual, text and verbal signals in the first second, with no logo or intro card. Facebook's hook window is sometimes described as 3-5 seconds versus 1-3 on Instagram, from a low-authority source ([OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/ideal-facebook-reels-length-format-retention)). Parent-tuned curiosity gaps from the notes include "This is why your 7-year-old counts on fingers (and why that's fine)" and "The 1,000-year-old reason we write numbers this way." No rigorous public study compares hook types.

### Shares come from practical value, positive arousal and identity

Berger and Milkman's analysis of about 7,000 NYT articles found positive, high-arousal content (awe, amusement) and practically useful content spread more, while low-arousal sadness suppressed sharing (academic, 2012) ([Journal of Marketing Research](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353)). Parents share to express pride and affection and to get support and advice, and mothers do more of the sharing (102 US parent interviews) ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11290302/)). For Math Dragons that maps to "try this at dinner tonight" techniques usable in under five minutes, content that lets a parent signal calm engagement ("math anxiety is contagious, here's how not to pass it on"), awe from math history, amusement from the dragon, and triggers tied to recurring moments such as homework time, report cards and Pi Day. No study isolates parents sharing educational short-form video.

### Cadence and cross-posting reward patience and clean masters

YouTube says time of day is "not something that we think about optimizing for" and that nothing in its systems rewards a daily upload quota (official, 2023) ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)). vidIQ's claim that channels with 200+ Shorts see steady growth has no sample size (3P) ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-shorts-algorithm/)), and Tubular Labs found more posts raise total views but can dilute per-video metrics (3P-data) ([NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/report-short-form-content-dominates-as-cross-category-collaborations-drive-engagement/)). A realistic solo cadence is 3-5 per week for 90+ days, judging each format after 10-15 posts. Cross-posting your own original video to both platforms is safe; the penalties target other apps' watermarks and re-uploads of others' content. Mosseri confirmed your own logo does not hurt reach while TikTok-watermarked Reels are kept out of recommendations (official, Instagram) ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-including-your-own-logo-on-a-reel-is-ok/730852/)), and YouTube watermarks Shorts downloaded from Studio ([TechCrunch, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/08/18/youtube-shorts-will-start-adding-watermarks-to-discourage-cross-platform-sharing/amp/)). Render one clean master, upload natively to each, and vary narration so the same gameplay never appears in near-identical videos. A claimed 2026 "10+ reposts in 30 days" penalty has no primary source ([Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/avoid-content-duplication-penalties-cross-posting-2026)).

| Platform | Primary per-video metrics | Working thresholds (3P, not platform targets) |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Viewed-vs-swiped %, engaged views, APV vs length, subs per 1K engaged views, shares | Beat channel median, then 70%+ viewed; APV 65%+ under 30s, 50%+ at 30-60s ([GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/blog/youtube-shorts-retention-rate-benchmarks)) |
| Facebook Reels | 3s/1-min views, avg watch time vs length, shares per reach, follower vs non-follower reach, follows | No reliable large FB-specific benchmark exists |

## Solvable puzzles and calm explainers are the proven faceless formats

### Case studies point to hands-only puzzles and adult audiences

The best-documented faceless math winners use **hands-only or screen-only puzzle solves framed as "can you solve this."** Andy Math grew from 5K subscribers (December 2022) to 100K (January 2024) to 400K (February 2025) after a single Short breakout in November 2023, having built a TikTok following first (fan wiki, unofficial) ([YouTube Fandom wiki](https://youtube.fandom.com/wiki/Andy_Math)), and now sits around 507K-541K subscribers with titles like "The Viral Yellow Triangle Challenge" and a soft CTA to a free site ([favoree.io](https://www.favoree.io/channel/andy_math-667c91dbe60bcc674c823020)). MindYourDecisions (slides plus voiceover) had about 3.12M subscribers in November 2024 ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCHnj59g7jezwTy5GeL8EA_g)) and grew by solving whatever math-bait was currently viral; one Facebook puzzle post drew 9.5M views and 46K+ comments (date unclear) ([Luckbox](https://luckboxmagazine.com/?p=25669)). TabletClass Math, a voice-over-whiteboard channel aimed at adults "returning to math," had about 985K subscribers in May 2026 and funnels viewers into paid courses ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCglNXzBo6bmKc9otKf-OuGQ/); [tcmathacademy](https://tcmathacademy.com/)), the clearest example of a faceless math audience converting to a product. No verifiable data exists on named AI-animated math pages on Facebook, and the report makes no claims about them.

Parent-heavy virality falls into three buckets. **Math bait** originated in parental homework: 48÷2(9+3) came from a Texas parent's son's homework in 2011 and split roughly 50/50, and a late-2024 tweet mocking a wrong answer to 6÷2(1+2) drew 77M views in three days ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/viral-math-problems-math-bait); [Know Your Meme editorial](https://knowyourmeme.com/editorials/whats-the-correct-answer-for-the-6212-equation-the-viral-math-bait-meme-explained)). **"New math vs old math"** is dominated by comedy and rants such as Nate Bargatze's Common Core bit ([TikTok discover](https://www.tiktok.com/discover/nate-bargatze-common-core-math)), which leaves calm, reassuring explainers under-served; educators note the odd strategies parents complain about are not in the standards themselves ([WeAreTeachers](https://www.weareteachers.com/common-core-math/)). **"Tricks they never taught us"** (9s on fingers, line multiplication) keeps recurring; Miss Chang's line-multiplication TikTok drew ~194.8K likes and spawned a part 2 ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@mathswithmisschang/video/6811187485255732486?lang=en)), and the disputed origin itself fuels comments ([NextShark](https://nextshark.com/japanese-multiplication-trick-is-the-best-first-thing-youll-learn-in-2021)).

Math Dragons should borrow the engagement mechanic of math bait without its deliberate ambiguity, which would sour trust for an education brand: pose genuinely solvable puzzles with a common wrong answer, aimed at parents to try with their kid ("Can your 2nd grader solve this dragon puzzle?"), and resolve them in a pinned comment or a part-2 video. Candidate recurring series from the notes are Dragon Puzzle Monday, Tricks They Didn't Teach Us, Homework Decoder, Math History in 30s and a Dad Dev Diary, which also fit YouTube's new series feature if confirmed.

### Gameplay works as the payoff, not the premise

There are few data-backed case studies of edu apps growing through faceless short-form. The clearest is a Prodigy TikTok slideshow (premise, build, classic in-game screenshot as payoff) that reached 1.1M views, about 1,417x the account's median, though its audience was nostalgic Gen Z former players ([Handler](https://gethandler.ai/outliers/prodigy-math-game-tiktok-slideshow/)). Prodigy also offers the brand's best contrast: a 2021 complaint to the FTC by Fairplay counted 16 membership ads and only 4 math problems in 19 minutes of gameplay ([Fairplay](https://fairplayforkids.org/prodigy-losing-equation/); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/child-protection-nonprofit-alleges-manipulative-upselling-math-game-prodigy-n1258294)). "No ads, no upsell pressure" is a differentiator parents can recognize, provided it stays strictly true. Indie PC games like Content Warning (1M+ copies in about two weeks) benefited from built-in clip export ([everything-pr](https://everything-pr.com/indie-games-viral-tiktok)), but a kid-facing share feature conflicts with a no-data kids' app; clips should come from parents or the developer only.

### Four value videos per promo, and promos that teach

The 80/20 value-to-promotion rule is repeated everywhere and sourced nowhere authoritative ([Enterprise Nation](https://learn-something.enterprisenation.com/learn-something/tech-hub-unlock-the-power-of-tiktok)). What the case studies do show is that faceless math channels sell through descriptions and off-platform links rather than in-video pitches ([favoree.io](https://www.favoree.io/channel/andy_math-667c91dbe60bcc674c823020)), and Duolingo reports that shared entertainment content drives new users directly ([Rival IQ](https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/duolingo-tiktok-marketing-strategy/)). Since most short-form views come from non-followers, every promo must also stand alone as value. The working ratio is roughly four reach or value videos per direct promo, with the promo itself built as a lesson where the app is the demo ("the making-ten trick, then here's how the dragon game drills it").

### The dragon can be the face if it has one consistent trait

Duolingo took its TikTok from about 50K to 16M+ followers by making the mascot the brand rather than a middleman, built on an exaggerated personality, trend-jacking and roasting in comments ([The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2025/02/25/duolingo-s-tiktok-mastermind-its-unhinged-social-strategy-and-killing-its-mascot); [Olson Zaltman](https://olsonzaltman.com/devilish-duo-why-brand-mascots-still-have-a-place/)). It did this with a 51-person marketing team ([Brand24](https://brand24.com/blog/duolingo-social-media-strategy/)), so the lesson transfers in shape, not scale. The Math Dragons dragon needs **one exaggerated, consistent trait** and a dry, parent-relatable humor register that matches the project's "talk to adults, not kids" rule. Duolingo-style guilt mechanics aimed at kids would contradict the "unstressed math" positioning and invite Prodigy-style criticism. The mascot also carries a compliance edge: YouTube lists animated characters as a Made for Kids factor, so the dragon must address adults (see the compliance section).

### Templated AI content is the dominant failure mode

YouTube's July 2025 rename of "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" and the pattern-based, channel-level enforcement that followed hit templated faceless channels hardest, including educational ones that "simply rephrase existing information" (3P, anecdotal cases) ([easyviral.ai](https://easyviral.ai/blog/why-90-percent-faceless-youtube-channels-fail-2026); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/faceless-youtube-channel-demonetized/)). Hallmarks of slop are repeated hooks, pacing and caption styles from shared templates ([air.io](https://air.io/en/top-of-voice/ai-on-youtube-in-2026-how-to-stay-ahead-without-turning-into-digital-noise)). The protections are rotating 3-4 format structures, an editorial perspective in every script, the developer's own reasoning and playtest observations, and a human pass on every video.

## A code-first pipeline guarantees correct math for $1-5 a video

### Voice: ElevenLabs by default, with an own-voice clone preferred

ElevenLabs' official API page (read 2026-10-19) lists **v3 at $0.08 per 1K characters**, Flash/Turbo at $0.04, and plans from Starter $6/month to Business $990/month ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api)); aggregators quote $0.10 or a $0.05 "v3 Conversational" tier, and the official page hints at a "v4" ([LLM Reference](https://www.llmreference.com/provider/elevenlabs/eleven-v3); [usagepricing.com](https://www.usagepricing.com/blueprint/activity/elevenlabs-2026-09-28-v3-conversational-dubbing-v2)). Commercial licensing starts at Starter ([fast.io](https://www.fast.io/resources/elevenlabs-ai-review-2026.md)). OpenAI gpt-4o-mini-tts runs about **$0.015/minute** with prompt-steerable delivery (July 2026 summary) ([techsy.io](https://techsy.io/it/blog/migliori-api-sintesi-vocale-sviluppatori)) and makes a reasonable fallback. Arena leaderboards changed leaders repeatedly in 2026 (Inworld, Gemini Flash TTS, Speechify Simba) ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)), so fit should drive the choice, not rank. A 45-second script is 600-800 characters, so cost is negligible. Cloning Justin's own voice has a double advantage: it gives an authentic "dad who built this" narrator and YouTube explicitly exempts "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs" from AI disclosure ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)). A calm, warm adult voice fits the audience; there is no sourced data on which voice styles parents prefer.

### Video models: short mascot hero shots only, never numbers

**Sora 2 is gone**: OpenAI's deprecation notice set the Videos API shutdown for 2026-09-24 with no replacement ([OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). The realistic shortlist for animating existing 2D dragon art is Kling 3.0 / O3 (element referencing for character lock, up to 15-second clips, ~$0.084-0.112/s official developer pricing), Veo 3.1 (up to three reference images "helpful for maintaining character consistency," $0.05/s Lite to $0.40/s standard, SynthID invisible watermark on all frames) and Runway Gen-4 Turbo/4.5 (~$0.05-0.12/s, unverified at source) ([fal.ai Kling 3](https://fal.ai/kling-3); [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-veo-3-1-and-new-creative-capabilities-in-the-gemini-api/); [InVideo, 2026-08-06](https://invideo.io/blog/ai-video-model-pricing/); [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/discover/blog/watermarking-ai-generated-text-and-video-with-synthid)). One aggregator warns the three Veo 3.1 *preview* model IDs on the Gemini API may shut down as early as **2026-10-22**, so GA IDs must be verified before coding ([benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/blog/posts/ai-video-generation-models)). No source has tested how well these models preserve a flat 2D illustrated style, so a hands-on bake-off with 3-5 style-bible images is required. The zero-model-risk alternative is rigging the existing layered game art (Rive or puppet/parallax in Remotion), which guarantees on-model consistency and aligns with the project's existing accessory-pipeline decision. At ~$0.10/s and 3-4 attempts per usable 5-second shot, each hero shot costs about $1.50-2.

### Programmatic animation is the math layer

Every shot showing digits, equations or UI must be rendered programmatically, because generative models garble text and a math brand cannot afford a wrong number. Manim (MIT, Python, the engine behind 3Blue1Brown) handles equations and number lines ([beginnersinai](https://beginnersinai.org/remotion-vs-motion-canvas-vs-manim/)). Remotion (React/TS) serves as the composition engine and is **free for individuals and companies of up to three people** ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing)). The structure that falls out is a `ShortTemplate` composition driven by a JSON spec (script beats, VO file, word timestamps, gameplay clip in/out points, Manim clip paths, hero clips, music) with an automated check that every number in the script matches the overlay data.

### Assembly, captions and capture stay in-house

CapCut has no official API and its terms ban scripted access (checked 2026-10-15) ([wireflow](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/capcut-api)), so it can only be a manual polish step. JSON-to-video APIs exist (Shotstack ~$0.20-0.40/min, JSON2Video ~$0.25/min, Creatomate variable and rising in 2026) ([wireflow Shotstack](https://www.wireflow.ai/blog/shotstack-pricing); [apispine](https://apispine.com/json2video/pricing); [motionbox, 2026-08-05](https://motionbox.io/blog/best-json2video-alternatives-in-2026)), but self-hosted Remotion is cheaper and more controllable. End-to-end generators like InVideo AI and Revid produce the generic stock look that reads as slop and cannot use the style bible or real gameplay ([marcandrews](https://marcandrews.com/invideo-ai-review-2026); [virvid](https://virvid.ai/blog/best-alternatives-revid-ai-top-5-tools-compared-2026)). Captions come from TTS or Whisper word timestamps rendered as 2-4 highlighted words inside the 9:16 safe zone, clear of the bottom ~20% and right-side UI; Submagic (~$69/month Business+API) is optional ([toolsforhumans](https://www.toolsforhumans.ai/ai-tools/submagic)). Gameplay capture should use `scrcpy --record` on a physical Android device, which streams to the PC and sidesteps the on-device `adb screenrecord` 3-minute cap ([scrcpy README](https://git.blob42.xyz/Archives/scrcpy/raw/commit/dca11f6c513e3b63e5494fce5bfffdc5c29be002/README.md); [Google Issue Tracker](https://issuetracker.google.com/issues/198237873)), driven by a Flutter `integration_test` script with a deterministic seed and debug banner hidden. The project's AVD uses NVK software rendering and will drop frames, so a physical device is preferred.

### Music: own soundtrack first, ElevenLabs Music second

ElevenLabs Music costs $0.15/minute via API with commercial use on Starter and above, and its training is licensed via Merlin and Kobalt ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing/api); [teamday, September 2026](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-music-models-2026)). Suno's terms (updated 2026-08-10) assign output ownership but promise no copyright, making Content ID shaky ([dev.to](https://dev.to/akaranjkar08/suno-songs-on-youtube-2026-content-id-and-disclosure-40p7)), and **Udio disabled all downloads on 2025-10-29** ([teamday](https://www.teamday.ai/blog/best-ai-music-models-2026)). The game's own soundtrack and SFX are owned IP and double as a signature sound. Never register AI music with Content ID, and keep a license receipt per track.

## Made for Kids is the compliance risk that can sink the funnel

### YouTube inauthentic content is a channel-level monetization policy

On **July 15, 2025**, YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content," calling it a minor clarification (official) ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)). The policy excludes from monetization "content that looks like it's made with a template," "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative," and channels "where content feels interchangeable from video to video"; it allows shared intros and series with a distinct concept per video, and allows AI tools when the final product reflects the creator's vision. The separate reused-content policy allows content that "mainly features the uploading creator," which describes first-party gameplay. Because the goal is beta recruitment rather than AdSense, losing YPP is lower-stakes here; whether inauthentic findings also reduce recommendations is plausible but unconfirmed by YouTube. Third-party reports of a January 2026 mass termination and a July 2026 three-category clarification could not be verified from primary sources ([Flocker](https://flocker.tv/posts/youtube-inauthentic-content-ai-enforcement/)).

### AI disclosure is required only for realistic content

YouTube requires disclosure for content that "seems realistic": real people saying things they didn't, altered real events, realistic scenes that didn't happen, and AI-generated music that is the main focus. It exempts clearly unrealistic or animated content, AI scripts, titles and thumbnails, and cloning one's own voice; it may auto-label content made with its own GenAI tools, carrying C2PA metadata, or detected by its systems, and those labels cannot be removed. Disclosure itself "doesn't limit a video's audience" or monetization (official) ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)). Cartoon dragons and gameplay need no label. A generic synthetic narrator is **ambiguous** (the page only exempts your own voice), but ticking "yes" puts a label only in the expanded description for non-photorealistic content, so voluntary disclosure is a cheap conservative default. Assets from C2PA-embedding tools such as OpenAI image generation may be auto-labeled regardless.

Meta may require an AI label on organic posts with photorealistic video or **realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally created or altered, auto-labels C2PA/IPTC signals, and gives a realistic AI-narrated reel as an example needing a label while a cartoon video does not (official, wording fetched via search summary) ([Meta Help](https://www.meta.com/en-gb/help/artificial-intelligence/1783222608822690/)). That makes disclosing an AI voice the safer default on Facebook; Meta has not stated whether labels affect distribution. Neither platform should ever see realistic AI children, parents or classrooms from this channel, which would trigger labels and endorsement-deception problems at once.

### Engagement bait differs sharply between platforms

Meta demotes engagement bait and clickbait (official, page updated July 2, 2025) ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/types-of-content-we-demote/)), with its five categories being vote, react, share, tag and comment bait, illustrated by examples like "Comment 'YES' if..." and "Tag friends who...," and with repeat offenders facing Page-level demotion; genuine questions are fine (2017 policy, via press) ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/facebook-demoting-engagement-bait/228071/)). YouTube explicitly says "it's okay to ask viewers to like, comment, or subscribe," and instead prohibits rewarding engagement, sub-for-sub schemes, forced engagement via polls, and maliciously misleading titles or thumbnails (official) ([YouTube spam policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)). This creates a real tension in the funnel: "Comment BETA and we'll DM the link" is a legitimate Meta automation pattern but structurally resembles comment bait, and Meta has not said how it treats it. Share prompts like "Tag your kid's teacher" from the shareability playbook are explicitly listed as tag bait on Facebook and must be rewritten as open invitations ("Send this to whoever does homework duty" is safer but untested). Giveaways tied to comments, shares or tags are out on both platforms.

### Made for Kids: the dragon must address adults

YouTube's MFK factors include subject matter such as early education, "characters... that appeal to children, including animated characters," child actors, language intended for children, and "simple songs or games, or early education"; no single factor decides, family-friendly is not automatically MFK, general-audience examples include "animated content that appeals to everyone" and "a family vlog for parents," and creators bear legal responsibility ("Do not rely on our systems to set your audience for you") (official) ([YouTube Help: MFK](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076?hl=en)). MFK videos lose comments, cards and end screens, the notification bell, personalized ads, save to playlist and more, and YouTube's ML can override a creator's setting with **one appeal per video** (official) ([YouTube Help: restricted features](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654?hl=en)). For a parent funnel that would be fatal. Mislabeling is also a legal exposure: Google/YouTube paid **$170M** in 2019 ([Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2019/09/05/youtube-coppa-violation/)), and Disney agreed to a **$10M** penalty announced September 2025 for setting "Not Made for Kids" at the channel level instead of reviewing videos, with finalization reported around January 2026 ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/?p=354788); [CyberInsider](https://cyberinsider.com/disney-fined-10-million-for-collecting-childrens-data-on-youtube/)). The amended COPPA Rule was finalized January 16, 2025, effective June 23, 2025, with a compliance deadline of **April 22, 2026** for most provisions (official) ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data); [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule)).

The defense is to make each video genuinely adult-directed: adult-addressed voiceover and on-screen text ("Parents: here's why your 7-year-old struggles with subtraction"), parenting and teaching pain points as the subject, gameplay framed as a demo for a decision-maker, parent/teacher/homeschool hashtags rather than kid tags, no sing-alongs or "count with me" segments, no kid-bingeable pure gameplay loops, and **a per-video audience review** rather than a blanket channel setting. Facebook has no MFK equivalent for organic Reels and requires users to be 13+; its risks are the general ones above ([Meta Transparency Center](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/types-of-content-we-demote/)). The safest approach to children on screen is none: hands-on-device shots and animated characters only, since child actors are an explicit MFK factor.

### FTC rules make "no data collection" an objective claim to substantiate

Promoting your own app on your own channel needs no paid-partnership disclosure, but any connected third party (a paid teacher, a friend, a relative) must disclose. The 2023 Endorsement Guides require endorsers to be bona fide users and require objective claims made through endorsements to carry the same substantiation as advertiser claims (official) ([FTC Endorsement Guides](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/p204500_endorsement_guides_in_2023.pdf); [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/advertising-marketing/endorsements-influencers-reviews)). FTC v. Lumos Labs (2016), a brain-training "improves performance" case, is the closest analog, though its details were not verified in the notes ([FTC year in review](https://www.ftc.gov/es/system/files/documents/public_statements/958883/160611yearinreview.pdf)). The resulting claims ladder runs from safe feature descriptions ("dragon-themed addition and subtraction practice," "no ads"), through moderate claims that need documentation ("designed around [standard]"), to risky unsubstantiated outcomes ("improves math scores," "proven," "learn 2x faster"). "No data collection" is the trap: Math Dragons uses Firebase anonymous auth and optional cloud sync, so the privacy claim must be worded to match exactly what Firebase and any SDK collect, or replaced with something precise like "no ads, no accounts required, no personal info asked."

### Music and EU notes

Meta's popular-music licenses generally cover personal, non-commercial use, so a business Page should use only the Sound Collection or owned/licensed audio (3P, consistent across sources) ([jeffbullas.com](https://www.jeffbullas.com/thread/what-are-the-rules-for-using-popular-music-in-facebook-stories/)), and law firms report waves of warning letters over business music use in the EU ([Osborne Clarke, March 2026](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/use-music-social-media-platforms-what-businesses-and-creators-need-know-about-usage)). EU AI Act Article 50 transparency obligations took effect **August 2, 2026** ([Al Jazeera, 2026-08-06](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/8/6/what-came-into-force-with-the-eus-ai-act-this-week-and-what-didnt)); deepfake disclosure falls on deployers and cartoon dragons are not deepfakes, so platform labels on any realistic AI audio cover the practical exposure. Sources conflict on a December 2, 2026 grace date for machine-readable marking ([compliancehub.wiki](https://compliancehub.wiki/eu-ai-act-article-50-transparency-digital-omnibus-2026/)).

## Every path to the beta runs through one typeable landing page

### Neither platform gives an organic one-tap link

Shorts descriptions and comments were delinked on **August 31, 2023** (secondary sources) ([link.boo](https://link.boo/guides/youtube-shorts-link-in-description)). The only in-player link is the **related video**, which shows "a clickable link below your channel handle" but must point to your own public or unlisted YouTube content and requires advanced feature access (official) ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14075157?hl=en)); it can only be set manually in Studio because the public API does not support it ([OpusClip help](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/link-shorts-to-original-video)). One anecdote puts related-video click-through at a few hundred per 100,000 views ([OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-to-long-form/)). A 2026 "Links on Shorts" feature exists only for brand-deal content via the Creator Partnerships API ([TRIBE](https://www.tribegroup.co/blog/youtube-links-on-shorts)). Channel links (up to 14) and long-form descriptions remain clickable ([Unilink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/how-to-add-link-youtube-shorts)). So the YouTube path is: every Short sets its related video to a 60-120 second long-form "Join the Math Dragons beta (iPhone + Android)" walkthrough with the landing URL in line one of its description, the landing URL sits first in channel links, and a short URL is spoken and shown in the Short.

On Facebook, Reel caption URLs are plain text for ordinary accounts. Meta confirmed a December 2025 test capping non-Verified Pages at 2 organic link posts per month, exempting links in comments ([TechCrunch, 2025-12-17](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/17/facebook-is-testing-a-link-posting-limit-for-professional-accounts-and-pages)). **Meta One** launched 2026-09-15 and sells clickable Reel links by tier, from none on Essential to 8 Facebook reels per month on Advanced and unlimited on Max (official) ([Meta Help](https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/960854640235758/)), at reported prices of $14.99 to $499/month ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/09/16/meta-one-brings-a-new-subscription-to-facebook-instagram-whatsapp/)). Meta's own reporting shows 98.7-98.9% of US Feed views in Q1-Q2 2026 had no external link ([Built by Foundry](https://www.builtbyfoundry.io/blog/how-many-links-can-you-post-facebook-2026-2-free-4-paid)). The free path is a first comment from the Page with the link, the landing URL in the Page's website field, and optionally keyword comment-to-DM through Meta Business Suite's free native template; Meta reportedly allows one private reply per comment within 7 days ([InstantDM](https://instantdm.com/blog/facebook-comment-automation)), ManyChat's free tier was gutted in March 2026 per a competitor ([ReplyRush](https://www.replyrush.com/post/best-comment-to-dm-automation-tools)), and FB Reels triggers have reliability complaints ([ManyChat Community](https://community.manychat.com/general-q-a-43/not-triggering-10609)). "First comment protects reach" is practitioner lore that Meta has never confirmed ([Socialync](https://www.socialync.io/blog/links-in-captions-vs-first-comment-2026)).

Inside the video, use one CTA, delivered both spoken and on screen, placed after the payoff in the final 2-5 seconds ([SuperDirector](https://superdirector.app/glossary/cta); [faceless.so](https://faceless.so/blog/short-form-video-script-the-retention-guide)), with at most a light on-screen mention early ("Free beta, link below"). No controlled data exists on CTA placement versus retention.

### The landing page must absorb store friction

TestFlight public links allow **up to 10,000 external testers**, need Beta App Review approval of the first build, can carry a tester cap and device criteria, and show testers anonymously (official) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/invite-external-testers)); builds expire **90 days** after upload (long-standing secondary sources) ([Thunkable](https://docs.thunkable.com/publish-to-app-store-ios/testflight-overview); [9to5Mac, 2017](https://9to5mac.com/2017/04/11/testflight-itunes-connect-updates-developers-beta-testers/)). Google Play closed testing admits only testers on an email list or in a Google Group ("Only members of that group can join"), requires a Google account, shows the opt-in link only once the app is Published (and it can take hours), and lets you put a feedback URL on the opt-in page; testers cannot post public reviews (official) ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334)). A stranger tapping the opt-in link from a Reel therefore cannot join unless the track uses an **open-join Google Group**, which turns Android into a three-step flow: join group, become a tester, install.

The landing page should detect platform, show the matching button large, explain TestFlight in one trust-building sentence ("Apple's official beta-testing app"), give desktop viewers a QR code, and offer an email signup for people not ready or on the wrong device. The current URL, apps.routeworks.app/math-dragons, is hard to type from a video; a short branded domain or path solves typeability without the spammy look of generic shorteners. Link-in-bio click-through is commonly put at 1-3% of profile visitors (weak vendor data) ([Hopp](https://www.hopp.co/post/understanding-ctr-and-clicks-on-your-link-in-bio)), so friction at each hop matters. Because neither TestFlight nor Play opt-in links carry UTMs, attribution must happen at the landing page (per-video paths such as /yt-012) plus a one-question "where did you hear about us?" in the welcome survey.

### The Play 12/14 rule rewards engaged testers, not signups

Personal developer accounts created after November 13, 2023 must run a closed test with **at least 12 testers opted in continuously for 14 days**; opting out and back in restarts that tester's count, and the production application asks about recruitment, feature usage, a feedback summary and the changes made, with insufficient engagement able to require more testing (official) ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465)). The threshold was 20 until December 2024 ([TestersCommunity](https://www.testerscommunity.com/blog/google-play-closed-testing-requirements-2026)). Recruit 25-40 Android testers so drop-offs never take the count below 12, ship a TestFlight build every 60-75 days so public testers never hit an expired build, and log feedback in a structured way from day one, since that log becomes the production application. Paid "12 testers" services satisfy the count but not the engagement questions ([Level Up Coding](https://levelup.gitconnected.com/the-12-tester-tax-google-plays-closed-testing-rule-is-quietly-killing-solo-devs-2cea83c2e338)).

### Keeping parent testers engaged

TestFlight collects screenshot, crash and general feedback in App Store Connect with webhooks available (official) ([Apple](https://www.developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/view-tester-feedback)) but does not notify you when it arrives ([newly.app](https://newly.app/how-to/testflight-feedback)). Twenty engaged testers beat a hundred "game crashed lol" reporters ([BetaHub](https://betahub.io/resources/how-to-run-closed-beta-test-discord/)). For Math Dragons, where the parent is the tester and the child is the user, that means stating the commitment up front ("play 3 times in 2 weeks, answer one 2-minute survey"), a parent-gated in-app prompt linking the same short form used as the Play feedback URL, a private "Founding Families" Facebook Group with weekly build notes and one question, and recognition incentives (opt-in credits, a beta-only cosmetic, a vote on the next dragon) that are never tied to reviews. No documented case study exists of an app recruiting beta testers through Shorts or Reels with conversion numbers, so Math Dragons should instrument its own funnel and become one.

## Conclusion

The research reframes what "viral" should mean for this channel. Raw views are now inflated by design on YouTube and partly paywalled from conversion on Facebook, and even a large hit sends only a trickle through a multi-hop funnel; meanwhile the Play production gate cares about a few dozen engaged parents, not a million viewers. The asset that actually compounds is trust with a specific adult audience, and the same choices that build it (adult-addressed voice, verified math, honest caveats, owned art and footage, a real developer behind it) are the ones that keep the channel out of Made for Kids, clear of inauthentic-content enforcement, and on the right side of the FTC. Compliance and craft point in the same direction here, which is unusual and worth exploiting.

The other notable implication is that accuracy can be a format. The myth-busting angle (Einstein, the nautilus, Gauss, "Japanese" multiplication, timed-test panic) lets a small faceless channel differentiate from both slop and rage-bait by being the calm, correct voice in a feed full of overclaims. The biggest unknowns are empirical rather than strategic: how a parent audience responds to an AI or cloned voice, how often a dragon-led video gets auto-classified as MFK, and what view-to-tester conversion looks like. All three can only be answered by instrumenting the first 30-60 videos.
