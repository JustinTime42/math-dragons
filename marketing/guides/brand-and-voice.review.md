# Accuracy and sense review: Brand and Voice Guide

## Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES

No math in the guide is wrong. Every arithmetic statement checks out in code, and the history myth-busts (Einstein, Gauss, base 60) hold up against the research notes. The privacy and offline claims about the app hold up against the code.

Five fixes are needed before approval. The first two are blocking:

1. **The Narrator's app sample line overclaims (claim 14; also 33-34).** "It notices which facts your kid already knows and spends the time on the ones they don't" is not true of all four games. Dragon Eggs never uses the adaptive engine. Dragon Runes uses it only to seed one fact per level. Even where the engine runs, it deliberately caps weak facts at 40% of picks. Proof point 5.2 "Adapts practice to the facts a child is still learning" has the same problem.
2. **The beta perk is worded three different ways (claims 45-46).** One version, "If Math Dragons ever adds paid features, every beta family gets them free," is open-ended. It would also cover the currency packs that PLAN.md lists as part of the planned model. Pick one literal promise.
3. **Section 4.4 says the sample lines are "all reviewed for accuracy."** That was false when it was written: the doc's own status line says it is awaiting review. Some lines also need changes (below).
4. **Tallyclaw contradicts himself about 7 x 8 (sense issue S1).** In 4.2 he says "No trick. Just respect." In 4.4 he says "Even I needed a trick for that one."
5. **The Narrator says "we" (sense issue S2).** "It's free while we test it" is the required beta line, yet 4.1 bans the Narrator from saying "we".

Em-dash count in the guide: **0**. Count of "not X, Y" reframes: **6**. That breaks the guide's own "max one per piece" rule if the guide counts as a piece. Only one is in a sample line, so the sample lines on their own are fine. Details in S9.

## Sense-read issues

Read as a skeptical parent and as a math teacher.

**S1. Tallyclaw contradicts himself on 7 x 8.** In 4.2 his calibration line is "Eight times seven is just fifty-six. No trick. Just respect." In 4.4 it is "Seven times eight. Fifty-six. Even I needed a trick for that one." Both can't be his stance. A teacher will also notice that "No trick" brushes off the real 5-6-7-8 mnemonic. *Fix:* keep the 4.4 line, which is warmer and true. Change the 4.2 example to something like "Don't tell the kid. Eight times seven gave me trouble for a century."

**S2. The Narrator says "we".** 4.1 says the Narrator speaks first person singular and is "never 'we' pretending to be a company". Yet 4.4, 5.4 and 6.1 make "It's free while we test it" the standard line. *Fix:* either say "It's free while I test it with families" or "It's free during the beta", or add a note to 4.1 that "we" is allowed when it means Justin plus the testing families.

**S3. The app sample line overclaims (claim #14).** A parent whose kid mostly plays Dragon Eggs will see random problems, not targeted ones. This is the kind of literal-truth failure the guide's own section 3.3 ("Honest") forbids. Blocking.

**S4. The beta perk has three wordings (claims #45-46).** "Full unlock free at launch", "paid unlock free at full release" and "ever adds paid features... gets them free" are three different promises. Under FTC Section 5 the broadest public wording is the one you are held to. Blocking.

**S5. Section 4.4 says "all reviewed for accuracy" before review (claim #37).** The guide that makes accuracy value #1 should not misstate its own review status.

**S6. The finger line could make a parent anxious.** "Most kids put them down on their own, around seven or eight" tells the parent of a 9-year-old who still uses fingers that their kid is late. That breaks the guide's own "Calm" value and the "no 'falling behind'" rule. *Fix:* "They tend to fade on their own as the facts stick."

**S7. Tallyclaw replying in comments (section 10) clashes with 4.2's "never invites kids to talk to him".** If a child comments, a mascot reply is a child-directed interaction and an MFK and COPPA signal. *Fix:* add "Tallyclaw never replies to a commenter who appears to be a child, and never asks commenters questions."

**S8. A teacher will notice the in-app world names.** Dragon Runes worlds are called "Inferno Algebra" and "Dragon's Calculus" (`dragon_runes_registration.dart`), but they cover the four operations. Any video or screenshot that shows them must not suggest the app teaches algebra or calculus. Consider flagging this to the app owner.

**S9. The guide breaks its own reframe rule.** Six "not X, Y" constructions: "Accuracy is part of the brand, not a tax on it" (3.1); "not a teacher at a whiteboard and not a hype host" (4.1); "Never 'we' pretending to be a company" (4.1, borderline); "Math is the game, not the toll gate" and "the gameplay, not a quiz" (5.3 #2); "Fingers aren't cheating. They're tools." (4.4). Only the finger line ships as copy, so the sample lines are compliant. But 5.3 #2 is itself an X/Y reframe, so any piece built on that core message has used up its one allowance before it starts. Worth knowing; trim the internal prose if Justin's rule covers docs too (his global rules say it does).

**S10. "Don't tell the kid" (4.2) is unclear.** It isn't clear what is being kept from the kid, and a parent might read it as secretive or a bit belittling. Under S1's fix it reads as "don't tell them I struggled", which works. Otherwise drop it.

**S11. The internal doc names a competitor.** "Duolingo-style" in 4.2 is fine internally, but 6.2 bans naming competitor apps negatively. Make sure it never gets copied into a script.

**S12. Stale and mismatched sources to fix alongside this guide.** PLAN.md:84 says playmathdragons.com is "not yet registered" (whois now shows it registered 2026-10-19). PLAN.md:76 says the site will use Umami for analytics but PLAN.md:83 says Cloudflare Web Analytics; the guide's "anonymous analytics" wording is safe either way, but the privacy copy will need one answer. The age range differs between store-listing (6-12) and the guide (5-12).

**S13. The release manifest asks for internet access (claim #29).** This is not a false claim. But "works offline, collects nothing" plus an Android INTERNET permission is the first thing a privacy-minded parent or reviewer will question. Recommend removing it from `android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml` (debug and profile builds keep their own) and dropping `NSAllowsArbitraryLoads`, so the claim survives inspection.
