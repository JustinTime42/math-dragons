# Compliance Checklist

The rules that keep the channel visible, the app in the stores' good books, and the brand honest. Every video runs this checklist three times:

1. **Script gate (Stage 3):** sections A-H on the treatment.
2. **Render gate (Stage 5):** sections A, B, C, D, E, F, G, H on the finished file (captions, on-screen text and edits can introduce problems the script didn't have).
3. **Upload:** section J, every time, on every platform.

Results go in the video's `review.md` next to the accuracy review. Anything unclear is a stop, not a guess: flag it for Justin.

Policies change. Each rule cites the dated research behind it; re-verify any rule older than six months before relying on it for a borderline call.

---

## A. Made for Kids (the biggest risk)

**Why it matters:** if YouTube treats a video as Made for Kids, it loses comments, end screens, cards, the notification bell and save-to-playlist, and YouTube's systems can override our setting with only one appeal per video. Animated characters, games that appeal to children, and early education are all on YouTube's list of factors, which describes us exactly. Disney paid $10M (announced September 2025) for setting "not made for kids" at the channel level instead of reviewing videos.

- [ ] The first spoken line or the on-screen text addresses an adult ("Parents:", "If your kid…", "Try this with your kid tonight").
- [ ] The subject is a parent or teacher concern, or math interesting to adults. Gameplay appears as a demo for a decision-maker, never as a kid-bingeable loop on its own.
- [ ] No child-directed language anywhere: no "Hey kids", "boys and girls", "can YOU solve it" aimed at a child, no count-alongs, no songs or sing-alongs.
- [ ] Tallyclaw speaks to adults only, and never invites a child to talk to him, play, or do something.
- [ ] No children on screen: no real kids, no kids' voices, no realistic AI-generated children. Hands, screens, illustration and Tallyclaw only.
- [ ] Title, description, tags and hashtags are parent/teacher/homeschool oriented. No kid-oriented tags.
- [ ] At upload, the audience is set **per video** to "No, it's not made for kids", after reading this section. Never rely on a channel default.
- [ ] If YouTube later marks a video Made for Kids: don't re-upload it. Note it in the metrics log, review what triggered it, and decide with Justin whether to use the single appeal.

## B. Claims (FTC, Apple 2.3.1, brand guide)

**Why it matters:** the FTC requires objective claims to be truthful and backed by evidence before they're made, and has acted against children's educational-benefit claims and false privacy-seal claims. Apple can act on misleading marketing made outside the App Store (guideline 2.3.1(a)).

- [ ] Every app claim is on the proof-point list in `brand-and-voice.md` §5.2 and is true **today**.
- [ ] **No adaptivity claims** ("adapts", "focuses on what your kid misses") until the app task ships: today Dragon Eggs and Fire Trail are random, Dragon Runes repeats one missed fact per level, and Dragon's Feast only nudges number choice (`brand-and-voice.md` §5.2).
- [ ] No outcome claims: "improves", "boosts scores", "proven", "learn faster", "makes kids smarter", "genius". Describe what the app does, not what it does to a child's future.
- [ ] Privacy is scoped: "The app collects no data" (never "we collect no data"; the website collects some). Never "COPPA compliant", never a seal or certification we don't hold, never "safe" without the specifics.
- [ ] The perk uses exactly: "Everyone in the beta gets the full unlock free when Math Dragons launches." Never tied to reviews, ratings, feedback content, follows or shares. Never called payment for testing.
- [ ] No testimonials unless they're from a real, named-with-permission adult who actually used the app, quoted accurately, and not stating outcomes (an outcome in a testimonial counts as our claim). Never AI-generated or composite "parents".
- [ ] Learning-science claims carry their confidence level from the research notes and pass the accuracy review (`marketing/README.md`).

## C. AI disclosure

**YouTube** requires the "altered or synthetic content" disclosure for content that seems realistic: real people saying things they didn't, altered real events, realistic scenes that didn't happen, and AI music that's the main focus. It exempts clearly unrealistic or animated content and **cloning your own voice**. Disclosure doesn't limit reach or monetization (YouTube Help, per the research). YouTube may add its own label automatically for content carrying C2PA metadata, and that label can't be removed.

**Meta** may require an AI label on photorealistic video or **realistic-sounding audio** that was digitally created or altered, and gives an AI-narrated reel as an example that needs a label.

Rules:
- [ ] **YouTube:** no disclosure needed for Justin's cloned voice, Tallyclaw, or animation. Tick the disclosure if the video contains any realistic AI person, place or event, AI music as the main focus, or any synthetic human-sounding voice that isn't Justin's clone.
- [ ] **Facebook:** turn on the AI label whenever the video uses any AI voice (including Justin's clone, which sounds realistic) or AI-generated realistic imagery. Meta's own example of a reel needing a label is one with a realistic AI voiceover, so treat this as required.
- [ ] Never make realistic AI children, parents, teachers or classrooms. (That would trigger labels and endorsement problems at once.)

## D. Engagement bait and spam

**Facebook demotes** vote, react, share, tag and comment bait ("Comment YES if…", "Tag a friend who…", "Share if you agree"), and repeat offenders get Page-wide demotion. **YouTube allows** asking viewers to like, comment or subscribe, but bans rewarding engagement, sub-for-sub schemes, and misleading titles or thumbnails.

- [ ] No "comment YES", "tag someone", "share if", "type 1 if" phrasing on Facebook. Genuine questions are fine ("What's your answer?" on a real puzzle).
- [ ] No giveaways, perks or rewards tied to likes, comments, shares, follows or subscribes, on either platform.
- [ ] "Comment BETA and we'll DM you the link" (keyword-to-DM automation): **not used** until Justin decides. Meta hasn't said whether it counts as comment bait, and the penalty is Page-wide.
- [ ] Share prompts are open invitations, never tagging: "Send this to whoever does homework duty" (untested), not "Tag your kid's teacher".
- [ ] Titles and thumbnails deliver what they promise. Puzzles are genuinely solvable; no deliberately ambiguous rage-bait (e.g. order-of-operations traps written to start fights).

## E. Music and audio

- [ ] Audio is one of: the game's own soundtrack and SFX, ElevenLabs Music on a plan whose current terms allow use in published social video (check the terms before first use; some plan versions restrict streaming media), Meta's Sound Collection (Facebook only), or other audio we have a license for, with the license or receipt saved in the video folder.
- [ ] Never trending commercial songs on the Facebook Page (Meta's popular-music licenses generally cover personal, not business, use).
- [ ] If AI music is the main focus of a video, tick YouTube's disclosure (section C).

## F. Visuals, IP and originality

YouTube's inauthentic-content policy (renamed July 15, 2025) removes monetization from channels whose videos look templated or interchangeable, and its October 1, 2026 update reduces reach for re-uploaded content. Meta has demoted reposts and minor edits since July 2025.

- [ ] Every video is a distinct concept with an editorial point of view, not a template refill. Series share a structure by design (YouTube allows series with a distinct concept per video), but a week's posts mix formats (`format-library.md`, to be written).
- [ ] A human (Justin) watches every video before it's published.
- [ ] Clean master uploaded natively to each platform. No other platform's watermark (never re-post a Short downloaded from YouTube Studio to Facebook). Our own logo is fine.
- [ ] Only our own art, gameplay, Tallyclaw, licensed fonts, or properly licensed assets. No other brands' characters or logos, no screenshots of competitor apps.
- [ ] No App Store, TestFlight or Google Play badges while the app is in beta. No ® in videos (Apple's symbols are for US-only materials and not for headlines); ® on first mention in website body copy only.
- [ ] Device frames, if any: Apple bezels only, and no iPhone and Android device side by side in one image.

## G. Kids, privacy and community

- [ ] No real child's name, face, voice, school or location, ever, including Justin's kids.
- [ ] Tester feedback quoted on screen only with the adult's written permission, first name or initial only, no child details beyond an age if they agree.
- [ ] Never show personal information from emails, comments or tester lists.
- [ ] **Comment moderation:** if a commenter appears to be a child, don't reply. Hide the comment if it contains personal information. Neither the Narrator nor Tallyclaw engages.
- [ ] Respond to comments as the Narrator (Justin), warm and brief, per `brand-and-voice.md` §10.

## H. Links and calls to action

- [ ] At most one CTA per video, after the payoff (value videos usually have none; the corner URL mark is branding). Spoken URL is always plain `playmathdragons.com`.
- [ ] Per-video paths (`/fb012`) only in clickable places: the Facebook first comment. YouTube attribution is per channel, not per Short: every Short's related video is the same walkthrough, whose description carries one path (e.g. `/yt`). Never spoken.
- [ ] No claims about the beta that aren't currently true (e.g. "join in 30 seconds"; the Android flow has three steps).

## I. Our own posts elsewhere, and fake engagement

- [ ] When Justin posts about Math Dragons anywhere else (parent Facebook groups, Reddit, comments on other channels), he says plainly that he built it. Never pose as a neutral parent (FTC Endorsement Guides).
- [ ] Never buy followers, subscribers, views, likes or reviews, and never use engagement-exchange schemes. The FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (effective October 2024) bans buying fake social-media indicators.

## I2. Paid partnerships (when they start)

- [ ] Anyone paid, gifted, or otherwise connected (a teacher, a friend, a relative) who posts about Math Dragons must disclose the connection clearly. Use the platforms' paid-promotion tools.
- [ ] Their objective claims need the same evidence as ours (FTC Endorsement Guides, 2023).
- [ ] Self-promotion on our own channel needs no paid-promotion disclosure.
