# MD-012: There are only 55 times-table facts

## Concept
Parents see that the 1-10 times table is 55 facts, not 100, once each flipped pair counts once, and only 36 after the easy ones and tens.

## Definition used (stated on screen)
- Tables **1 through 10** (1 × 1 up to 10 × 10). No zeros table.
- A fact and its flip (3 × 7 and 7 × 3) count as **one** fact.
- Squares (7 × 7) are included, once each.
- 55 = all such facts. 36 = the same count with every fact that has a 1 or a 10 removed (tables 2 through 9 only).

## Hooks
- A: First frame: a 10 × 10 grid of facts, the lower-left half already fading. On-screen text: "Not 100 facts. 55." VO (Narrator): "Parents, your kid doesn't have a hundred times-table facts to learn."
- B: First frame: the grid shrinking to a small cluster in the middle. On-screen text: "The times table is smaller than you think". VO (Narrator): "Parents, your kid's times table is about half the size it looks."

## Math strings (exact, rendered from code)
Verified by running `python3 -I verify.py` (scratchpad, 2026-10-27): ordered pairs 1-10 = 100; unordered pairs with squares = 55 (10 squares + 45 flipped pairs); unordered pairs containing a 1 or a 10 = 19; tables 2-9 unordered = 36. For reference (not used): 0-10 gives 66, 1-12 gives 78.
- `Not 100 facts. 55.`
- `1 × 1 to 10 × 10 = 100 boxes`
- `3 × 7 = 7 × 3`
- `3 × 7 = 7 × 3 = 21`
- `Each pair once: 55`
- `1 × 7 = 7`
- `10 × 7 = 70`
- `× 1 stays the same`
- `× 10 adds a 0`
- `55 − 19 = 36`
- `36`
- Grid cells: all 100 products a × b for a, b in 1..10, generated by code (never typed)
