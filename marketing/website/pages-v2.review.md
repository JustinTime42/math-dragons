## Sense read

As a **privacy-conscious parent**:
- **S1. The old policy made a promise this deploy has to keep.** The live policy says "If we ever add analytics, we will update this policy first and respect those signals." Ship the new policy first (with `websiteId` empty), then set the website ID in a later deploy. Cheap, and it makes the promise literally true.
- **S2. "We collect only your email address" followed by "and the IP address" reads like a catch.** This is the line a careful parent screenshots. Fix #14.
- **S3. "We never share... your address" vs. "run for us by Buttondown".** Fix #13 so the form and policy say the same thing.
- **S4. On `/feedback`, "your beta thank-you" is undefined.** `/` and `/support` explain it with the perk sentence; `/feedback` doesn't. Link it: "help with your <a href="/support">beta thank-you</a>". Don't paraphrase the perk (brand §5.4).
- The opt-out link label changing to "Visits not counted (your browser asked)" is clear and kind. Good.

As an **app-store reviewer**:
- The app section is unchanged and still accurate; the tests pin it. The page the app links to (`/feedback`, behind the parent gate) now has an email form and analytics. That is website data, not app data, and the policy keeps the two apart, so the App Privacy label ("Data Not Collected") and Play Data safety form stay correct. No action, but don't add anything aimed at children to `/feedback`.
- Apple and Google need retention and deletion stated: the website section now does that, once #17 is fixed.

As an **FTC staffer**:
- **S5. The "I'm 18 or older" checkbox isn't a COPPA age screen, and the copy shouldn't lean on it.** FTC COPPA FAQ H.3: "simply including a check box stating, 'I am over 12 years old' would not be considered a neutral age-screening mechanism." On a general-audience, parent-directed site, COPPA doesn't require asking age at all (H.1, A.12), so the box is fine as a signal of who the list is for. The real protections are the parent-directed posture (SPEC §2), double opt-in, deleting unconfirmed addresses (C8) and prompt deletion on actual knowledge (C6, C9). The current copy doesn't call it a COPPA measure; keep it that way. If the FTC ever treated the site as mixed-audience (dragon art is a named factor), a yes/no box would not count as the neutral screen; that is a legal call for Justin, not a copy fix.
- Every promise in the policy that depends on a vendor setting (#1, #7, #17, #19, #20) is an FTC Act Section 5 deception risk if the setting drifts. Re-check C1-C4 and C6-C8 at each policy update and after any Buttondown or Cloudflare plan change.
- **S6. EU/UK visitors:** the policy has no lawful basis, data-transfer note (Umami, Buttondown and Cloudflare are not stated to be EU-hosted) or right-to-complain line. The UK's analytics exemption only needs clear information and a way to object, which the footer link gives. For GDPR, SPEC §7 deliberately chose US-first; flag only. If EU traffic grows, add a short "If you're in the EU or UK" paragraph (legal basis: legitimate interests for counting visits, consent for the list; your rights; how to complain).

Minor wording:
- §4 runs about grade 8. The #4 and #5 rewrites shorten the two longest sentences.
- `launch_list_submit` counts button clicks, not signups (#31). Use Buttondown's confirmed count for signups.
