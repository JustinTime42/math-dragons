## Verdict: PASS WITH FIXES

The app policy is accurate against the tester build: every storage, link, gate, network and purchase claim checks out in code. It still covers who we are, what is and isn't collected, third parties, retention and deletion, contact and children. But the split dropped one thing the app policy needs: **what happens to emails parents send us** (feedback and support, which the app's own parent-gated `/feedback` page asks for, including the child's age and device model). That paragraph (Zoho Mail, 12-month retention) used to be on the app-linked page and now lives only on the website policy. Two smaller split issues: the home page's "privacy policy" link under the launch list form now points at the app policy, and the app policy's sharing line got weaker than the live one. The website policy is self-contained and matches the code, but two fixes from the prior review (#16, #20) are still not applied and CalOPPA's "other parties tracking across sites" disclosure is missing.

Mechanical checks (built HTML, scripts stripped): zero em-dashes, zero en-dashes on both pages. Banned §6.2 words ("safe", "proven", "guaranteed", "COPPA", "lifetime", "forever", "we collect no data", "anonymous"): zero hits. No "That's not X, that's Y" reframes.

## Sense read

**As a parent tapping "Privacy policy" inside the app (after the multiplication check):** the page opens with the short version in bold, which is exactly what a parent wants. Clear and calm. Two things would trip a careful parent: (1) the page invites email three times but never says what happens to that email, even though the feedback link right next to it asks for my kid's age (#8); (2) the intro says the app "doesn't send anything anywhere", then the beta section says Apple and Google may share crash reports. That's true (the OS sends them, not the app), but one clause would remove the doubt: "(these come from Apple's and Google's own systems, not from the app)". The beta-note bullet reads like a hedge ("can get"); use the exact perk line (#2).

**As an Apple or Google reviewer comparing with "Data Not Collected" / Data safety "no data collected":** consistent. Storage is on-device only, no network permission on Android, no SDKs, links gated and opening in the browser, no purchases. The policy names the developer, has a privacy contact, covers retention and deletion, and children's privacy. The split helps here: no website analytics text a reviewer could misread as app tracking. The reviewer could ask about retention of beta feedback and emails, which #7 and #8 answer. Apple 1.3 is met as written; the Umami counting on the destination pages is outside the app, but saying those pages have their own policy (#9) closes the question before it's asked.

**As a privacy-conscious parent reading the website policy:** complete on its own: what's counted, how to stop it, what the list keeps, who processes it, how long, how to delete. The one confusing moment is on the home page, where the link under the form sends me to the app policy (#23). The "Every email has an unsubscribe link" and "we delete the address" lines are slightly over-promised (#17, #18). Missing a plain "no one follows you to other sites" line, which CalOPPA wants and parents like (#19). Reading level is about grade 8 in the Umami paragraphs (unchanged from the reviewed text); acceptable for a policy.

**Carried over, still open:** prior review config C1-C12 (no-cookies DevTools check, Buttondown tracking off, hard deletion, cleanup, hosted form off, Umami Hobby plan, deploy policy before setting `websiteId`). Every vendor-dependent sentence in the website policy is only true once those are done.
