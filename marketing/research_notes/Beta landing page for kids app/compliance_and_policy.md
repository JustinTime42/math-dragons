## Q1. COPPA as applied to the website: is a parent-directed marketing site "directed to children"? What keeps it parent-directed? What if a child submits the contact form?

### Takeaway
COPPA uses a totality-of-the-circumstances, multi-factor test. Since the 2025 amendments (effective June 23, 2025; full compliance due April 22, 2026), "marketing or promotional materials or plans", "representations to consumers or to third parties", and "reviews by users or third parties" are named evidence of intended audience. A site written for parents, with no kid-facing activities, can stay outside "directed to children". The risk is the dragon characters, which hit the "animated characters" factor. If a child emails through the contact form, the one-time-response exception lets the developer reply once and then delete promptly.

### Cited Findings
- **Rule text (16 CFR 312.2, current eCFR as mirrored by Cornell LII, amended text):** "Website or online service directed to children means a commercial website or online service, or portion thereof, that is targeted to children." — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **Factors (verbatim):** "the Commission will consider its subject matter, visual content, use of animated characters or child-oriented activities and incentives, music or other audio content, age of models, presence of child celebrities or celebrities who appeal to children, language or other characteristics of the website or online service, as well as whether advertising promoting or appearing on the website or online service is directed to children. The Commission will also consider competent and reliable empirical evidence regarding audience composition and evidence regarding the intended audience, including marketing or promotional materials or plans, representations to consumers or to third parties, reviews by users or third parties, and the age of users on similar websites or services." — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **"Portion thereof":** the definition covers a child-directed *portion* of a site, so a kids' corner on an otherwise parent site could be child-directed on its own. — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **Linking safe harbor (verbatim):** "A website or online service shall not be deemed directed to children solely because it refers or links to a commercial website or online service directed to children by using information location tools, including a directory, index, reference, pointer, or hypertext link." So linking to the App Store or Play listing does not make the site child-directed by itself. — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **Mixed audience (verbatim, partial):** a mixed-audience site is one "that is directed to children under the criteria set forth in paragraph (1)... but that does not target children as its primary audience, and does not collect personal information from any visitor... prior to collecting age information or using another means that is reasonably calculated... to determine whether the visitor is a child." — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **The 2025 amendments added the four evidence examples** (marketing/promotional materials or plans, representations to consumers or third parties, user/third-party reviews, age of users on similar services). The FTC said it does not expect operators to continuously monitor all such information. — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-coppa-rule-changes-in-the-biden); [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/ftc-announces-new-childrens-privacy-requirements-updated-coppa-rule) (commentary)
- **Dates (commentary):** published in the Federal Register April 22, 2025; effective June 23, 2025; full compliance by April 22, 2026. — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2025/05/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule). The FTC FAQ page banner states: "The COPPA Rule was amended on April 22, 2025." — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- **FTC FAQ D.3:** a site "will not be considered 'directed to children' just because some children visit your site." **H.5:** "no single factor is determinative." **D.4:** "a general audience site does not become 'mixed audience' just because some children use the site or service." — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- **COPPA covers only data collected from children. FAQ F.4:** "COPPA only covers information collected online from children." It "does not cover information collected from adults that may pertain to children." FAQ F.4 also treats a "parent's corner" as a non-child-directed portion of an otherwise child-directed site. — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- **"Personal information" includes persistent identifiers (verbatim):** "A persistent identifier that can be used to recognize a user over time and across different websites or online services. Such persistent identifier includes, but is not limited to, a customer number held in a cookie, an Internet Protocol (IP) address, a processor or device serial number, or unique device identifier." — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **Limits on the "support for internal operations" exception (verbatim):** information collected for internal operations "cannot be used or disclosed to contact a specific individual, including through behavioral advertising, to amass a profile on a specific individual, or for any other purpose." — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **"Online contact information" (verbatim):** "an email address or any other substantially similar identifier that permits direct contact with a person online..." — [16 CFR 312.2 (LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/312.2)
- **If a child submits the contact form. FAQ I.2:** the one-time exception applies where the sole purpose is "to respond directly on a one-time basis to a specific request from the child" and the information "is deleted by the operator from its records promptly after responding to the child's request." **FAQ H.4:** "you must delete the child's online contact information from your records promptly after you send your response"; if you choose not to respond, "you must still immediately delete the child's personal information from your records"; and such an email "may give you actual knowledge that you have collected personal information from a child." — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)

### Inferences
- **The likely regulatory view:** COPPA attaches mainly to the *app*, which is plainly child-directed. The app collects nothing, so COPPA obligations there are minimal. The *site* is a separate question under the same factors. Parent-focused copy (origin story, "for parents", FAQ, privacy), no games, and no child-oriented incentives point toward a general-audience or parent-directed site.
- **Factors that cut the other way:** dragon characters (the "animated characters" factor), bright kid-style visuals, kid-voiced audio or video, and child actors in videos (the "age of models" factor). Under the 2025 evidence language, promo videos placed on kid-heavy platforms, or "send your kid here" copy, could also count as evidence of intended audience.
- **Design choices that keep the site parent-directed (inference, not FTC-prescribed):**
  - Write the copy to adults ("your child", not "you'll love it!").
  - Use the characters as illustration, not as interactive guides. No "talk to the dragon" or "say hi" prompts.
  - No playable demos, puzzles, quizzes, coloring pages, or rewards for kids on the site.
  - Use adult or parent testimonials only.
  - Label forms "For parents and teachers".
  - Keep any SEO articles parent- and educator-oriented.
  - Don't run ads for the site on child-directed placements.
- **Contact form handling:** add a line like "This form is for parents and adults. If you're under 13, ask a parent to write to us." If a message is evidently from a child, reply once if needed and then delete it, along with any copies in the mailbox or form backend. Don't add it to any list. A neutral age question isn't required for a parent-directed site, but it would help if the site ever drifts toward mixed audience.
- **Waitlist email capture:** this is collection of online contact information. On a parent-directed site it is ordinary adult marketing data. If the site were child-directed, a waitlist email from a child would need verifiable parental consent, because it isn't a one-time response. That is one more reason to keep the site clearly parent-directed.
- **Cookieless analytics and COPPA:** COPPA's definition counts an IP address as personal information if the site is child-directed. Aggregate analytics with no profiling plausibly fall under "support for internal operations", but only if the site is actually child-directed. On a parent-directed site the question doesn't arise.

## Q2. Privacy policy requirements: Apple, Google Play, CalOPPA, state laws. Must the policy cover the website too?

### Takeaway
Both stores require a publicly reachable privacy policy URL that accurately describes the app's data practices, and both require one even when the app collects nothing. Google additionally requires a non-PDF, non-geofenced, non-editable web page with a privacy contact and a retention/deletion policy. Apple requires a Support URL with a working contact method. Neither store requires the policy to cover the *website*. But the site will collect data (contact form, waitlist, analytics), and CalOPPA requires a conspicuously posted policy for any site collecting PII from California residents. The practical answer is one policy that covers both "the app" and "the website" in separate sections.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 5.1.1(i) (verbatim):** "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner. The privacy policy must clearly and explicitly: Identify what data, if any, the app/service collects, how it collects that data, and all uses of that data. Confirm that any third party with whom an app shares user data … will provide the same or equal protection of user data… Explain its data retention/deletion policies and describe how a user can revoke consent and/or request deletion of the user's data." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 1.5 Developer Information (verbatim):** "People need to know how to reach you with questions and support issues. Make sure your app and its Support URL include an easy way to contact you; this is particularly important for apps that may be used in the classroom. Failure to include accurate and up-to-date contact information not only frustrates customers, but may violate the law in some countries or regions." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 1.3 Kids Category (verbatim excerpt):** "These apps must not include links out of the app, purchasing opportunities, or other distractions to kids unless reserved for a designated area behind a parental gate." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 5.1.4(a) (verbatim):** "Apps intended primarily for kids should not include third-party analytics or third-party advertising." **5.1.4(b):** "Apps not in the Kids Category cannot include any terms in app name, subtitle, icon, screenshots or description that imply the main audience for the app is children." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Google Play User Data policy (verbatim/near-verbatim):**
  - "All apps must post a privacy policy link in the designated field within Play Console". A link or text is also required within the app.
  - The policy must be on an "active, publicly accessible and non-geofenced URL (no PDFs) and is non-editable."
  - Required contents: developer information plus "a privacy point of contact or a mechanism to submit inquiries", data types accessed, collected, used, and shared, secure handling, and "The developer's data retention and deletion policy". It must be labeled as a privacy policy.
  - "Apps that do not access any personal and sensitive user data must still submit a privacy policy."
  - "All developers must complete a clear and accurate Data safety section for every app."
  
  — [Google Play User Data policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311)
- **Google Play Families policy (verbatim excerpts):**
  - "Apps must have a privacy policy that accurately reflects data collection and handling practices."
  - "You must disclose the collection of any personal and sensitive information from children in your app."
  - "Apps that solely target children must not transmit Android advertising identifier (AAID)…"
  - Teacher Approved: "All apps that comply with Google Play Families policies can opt in to be rated for the Teacher Approved program" (inclusion not guaranteed).
  
  The page shows no effective date and says nothing about where the policy must be hosted. — [Google Play Families policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9893335)
- **CalOPPA (Bus. & Prof. Code 22575-22579), per commentary:**
  - It requires operators of sites and online services that collect PII from California residents to conspicuously post a privacy policy.
  - AB 370 (2013) added (b)(5): disclose how the operator responds to browser Do Not Track signals. Commentators read the DNT disclosure as tied to tracking across third-party sites, and as a disclosure duty, not a duty to honor the signal.
  - (b)(6): state whether third parties may collect PII through the site.
  - (b)(7): an alternative is to link to a "program or protocol" offering choice.
  - Liability attaches for knowing and willful, or negligent and material, failures.
  
  — [Alston & Bird privacy blog](https://www.alstonprivacy.com/california-adopts-do-not-track-disclosure-law-a-b-370-amends-the-california-online-privacy-protection-act-caloppa-to-require-new-privacy-policy-disclosures-for-websites-online-services-and-mobile/); [CommLawBlog](https://www.commlawblog.com/2013/10/articles/internet/new-california-online-privacy-requirements-have-nationwide-reach/); [IAPP](https://www.iapp.org/news/a/what-do-the-new-disclosure-requirements-under-caloppa-mean-for-your-busines) (all commentary; the oag.ca.gov page returned 403)

### Inferences
- **Must the policy cover the site?** The store rules only require coverage of the app ("app/service"). CalOPPA, GDPR transparency (for EU visitors), and the FTC's truthfulness standard all apply to the site's own collection. Recommended structure:
  1. Who we are and how to contact us (named contact or email).
  2. The Math Dragons app: what it collects (nothing), no ads, no accounts, no third-party SDKs (list any crash-reporting SDKs if they exist), and children's privacy/COPPA statement.
  3. The website: contact form data, waitlist email, cookieless analytics vendor, hosting/CDN logs (IP addresses), and retention periods.
  4. Do Not Track response. Example: "The site does not track visitors across third-party sites, so we do not respond differently to DNT signals."
  5. Third parties that may collect data via the site (analytics vendor, form processor, email service provider).
  6. Retention and deletion, and how to request deletion.
  7. Effective date and how changes are notified.
- **Moving the policy from the older site:** update the URL in both App Store Connect and Play Console, keep the old URL redirecting (301), and keep the page non-PDF, non-geofenced, and not user-editable (Google). Make sure the in-app link points to the new URL in the next build.
- The Support URL needs a working contact method (Apple 1.5). The contact page or form on the site can serve as the Support URL.
- **Data safety / App Privacy "no data collected":** the declarations must match reality, including any SDKs (crash reporting, Flutter plugins). This is about the app, not the site.

## Q3. Tester incentives: "lifetime free access", cosmetic rewards, incentivized reviews

### Takeaway
Both Apple and Google flatly prohibit incentivized ratings and reviews. Apple's TestFlight rule bars distributing betas "in exchange for compensation of any kind". The plain reading is a bar on testers *paying* (or crowdfunding backers receiving the build as a reward), not on the developer giving thank-you perks. Rewarding *testing* (not reviews) with cosmetic items or lifetime access isn't explicitly prohibited by either store in the text retrieved, but it is ambiguous. Never tie any perk to leaving a rating or review, or to its sentiment.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple 2.2 Beta Testing (verbatim):** "Any app submitted for beta distribution via TestFlight should be intended for public distribution and should comply with the App Review Guidelines. Note, however, that apps using TestFlight cannot be distributed to testers in exchange for compensation of any kind, including as a reward for crowd-sourced funding." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple Section 3 intro (verbatim):** "If we find that you have attempted to manipulate reviews, inflate your chart rankings with paid, incentivized, filtered, or fake feedback, or engage with third-party services to do so on your behalf, we will take steps to preserve the integrity of the App Store, which may include expelling you from the Apple Developer Program." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 3.2.2(x) (verbatim):** "Apps must not force users to rate the app, review the app, download other apps, or other store-related actions in order to access functionality, content, or use of the app. Apps may otherwise incentivize users to take specific actions within apps (such as by completing a level or watching an ad)." — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Apple 2.3.1(a) (verbatim excerpt):** "marketing your app in a misleading way, such as by promoting content or services that it does not actually offer … or promoting a false price, whether within or outside of the App Store, is grounds for removal…". This is relevant if "lifetime free" is promised and later contradicted. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- **Google Play Ratings, Reviews, and Installs (verbatim):**
  - "We prohibit any manipulation of ratings, reviews, or install counts. This includes incentivizing feedback, using forced pop-ups, or paying for fake reviews."
  - Violation example: "Asking users to rate your app while offering an incentive."
  - "Don't offer rewards or incentives for ratings or reviews."
  
  The page contains no text about tester incentives. — [Google Play policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898684)

### Inferences
- **Ambiguity:** Google's "incentivizing feedback" could be read broadly enough to cover rewarding *private* tester feedback. In context, it sits under ratings/reviews manipulation and most naturally means public store feedback. Flag this. The safest framing is "thank-you for beta testers" (granted to everyone in the beta regardless of what they say), never "leave a review to get X".
- **Apple's "compensation of any kind"** most plausibly targets testers paying for access, e.g. Kickstarter-backer rewards. A developer-to-tester perk isn't compensation *paid by* testers. Still, avoid structuring beta access as a reward for anything purchased (e.g. "buy the plush, get the beta").
- **"Lifetime free access":** the app is currently free with no IAP, so this promise has no store mechanics today. If monetization arrives later, honoring it on iOS would need to run through Apple's systems (offer codes, or a non-consumable unlocked via StoreKit). An honor-system unlock outside IAP could conflict with Apple 3.1.1 **[not re-verified this session]**. Word it so it is truthful and deliverable (e.g. "Beta testers will get [specific cosmetic set] free when it launches"), or the 2.3.1(a) misleading-marketing concern applies.
- **FTC overlay:** the FTC's Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (16 CFR Part 465, effective October 2024) prohibits incentives conditioned on review sentiment, and the FTC Endorsement Guides require disclosure of material connections **[not re-verified this session]**. Testers who post about the app after receiving perks should disclose it.

### Gaps
- The FTC Consumer Reviews Rule and the Endorsement Guides (16 CFR 255, revised 2023) were not fetched this session.
- Found no official Apple or Google text that addresses developer-granted perks to beta testers directly.

## Q4. Cookies and consent; contact form and email capture

### Takeaway
- **US:** no general cookie-banner law. Disclosure in the privacy policy (CalOPPA) is enough.
- **EU:** ePrivacy Art. 5(3) can apply to cookieless JavaScript analytics, because the EDPB's final 2024 guidelines treat script-instructed collection as "gaining access". Whether consent is needed then depends on whether an exemption applies, and the EDPB didn't decide that. Many EU DPAs tolerate strictly first-party, aggregate audience measurement, but there is no EU-wide safe harbor.
- **UK:** since February 5, 2026, the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 exempts first-party statistical-analytics cookies from consent, provided users get clear information and a means to object.
- **Email capture:** CAN-SPAM in the US (opt-out model). In the EU and UK, consent is effectively required for marketing email to individuals. Double opt-in is a norm and good evidence of consent, not a legal mandate in most places.

### Cited Findings
- **EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 on the technical scope of Art. 5(3) ePD, v2.0 adopted October 7, 2024.** Para. 51 (verbatim): "In the case of dynamically constructed tracking pixels, it is the distribution of the applicative logic (usually a JavaScript code) that constitutes the instruction. As a consequence, it can be considered that the collection of identifiers provided through such tracking mechanisms constitutes a 'gaining of access' in the meaning of Article 5(3) ePD." — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- **EDPB para. 53 (verbatim):** "The fact that this information is being produced locally does not preclude the application of Article 5(3) ePD." **Para. 55:** "Unless the entity can ensure that the IP address does not originate from the terminal equipment of a user or subscriber, it has to take all the steps pursuant to the Article 5(3) ePD." — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- **EDPB para. 56 (verbatim):** "the applicability of this article does not systematically mean that consent needs to be collected. The EDPB thus reminds that in each case it would have to be assessed if a consent is needed or whether an exemption under Article 5(3) ePD could apply." — [EDPB Guidelines 2/2023 v2](https://edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202302_technical_scope_art_53_eprivacydirective_v2_en_0.pdf)
- **UK DUAA 2025:**
  - Section 112 inserts Schedule A1 into PECR, exempting cookies used solely for statistical information about service use with a view to improvement.
  - Users must be given clear information and "a means of objecting".
  - Commencement of the main provisions was February 5, 2026 (SI 2026/82). One vendor source instead says the Act was fully in force June 19, 2026, which appears to refer to the final tranche (the complaints procedure).
  - Advertising remains consent-based.
  - PECR fines now align with UK GDPR (up to £17.5m or 4% of turnover).
  
  — [Bratby Law](https://bratby.law/data-use-and-access-act-2025-commencement/); [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/data-use-and-access-act-2025-duaa-compliance/); [Harper James](https://harperjames.co.uk/news/what-the-data-use-and-access-act-means-for-marketers-and-website-cookies/) (commentary, not statutory text)
- **Cloudflare Web Analytics, official claim:** it does not use "client-side state, like cookies or localStorage, for tracking users" and does not "fingerprint" individuals via IP or User-Agent. A "visit" is a page view whose referer doesn't match the hostname. — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/free-privacy-first-analytics-for-a-better-web/)
- **Conflicting view:** an independent technical review reported the beacon set a cookie with a unique identifier, and noted that Cloudflare's privacy policy says it may log end-user IPs. The review's age is unknown, and it predates Cloudflare's deprecation of `__cfduid` in May 2021. — [ctrl.blog review](https://www.ctrl.blog/entry/review-cloudflare-analytics/)

### Inferences
- **US:** no banner needed for cookieless analytics. Disclose the analytics vendor, what it collects (page URL, referrer, coarse location, user agent), and that no cross-site tracking occurs. That also covers the CalOPPA DNT and third-party disclosures.
- **EU:** under the EDPB reading, even cookieless JS beacons engage Art. 5(3). Whether consent is needed turns on national implementations of the "strictly necessary" exemption. A small, parent-directed US site with no EU targeting has low practical exposure. Options, from least to most protective:
  1. Server-side or edge log analytics with no client JS. These are outside Art. 5(3) per the guidelines' terminal-equipment logic (inference), though the GDPR still applies to IP processing.
  2. Cookieless first-party analytics plus a clear notice and an opt-out link.
  3. A consent prompt for EU visitors only.
  
  Don't call any of this "GDPR-exempt".
- **UK:** cookieless first-party analytics with a privacy notice and an opt-out fits comfortably within the new DUAA exemption.
- **Children and the UK (ICO Children's Code / Age Appropriate Design Code):** the Code applies to online services "likely to be accessed" by children in the UK **[not re-verified this session]**. A parent-directed marketing site is arguably not likely to be accessed by children, but kid-styled dragon art pushes the other way. Mitigation: high-privacy defaults (already met by having no tracking and no profiling).
- **Contact form:**
  - Collect the minimum (name optional, email, message).
  - State the purpose ("to reply to you").
  - Set a retention period (e.g. delete after 12 months, or after the issue is resolved).
  - Don't add contacts to the waitlist without a separate, unticked opt-in.
  - Add the "for adults" note from Q1.
- **Waitlist (US, CAN-SPAM [not re-verified this session]):** commercial emails need accurate headers and subject lines, identification as an advertisement unless the recipient opted in, a valid physical postal address (a P.O. box or registered commercial mail receiving agency works for a solo developer), a working opt-out, and opt-outs honored within 10 business days. Pure transactional or relationship messages are mostly exempt.
- **Waitlist (EU/UK):** freely given, specific, unticked-box consent, a record of consent, and an easy unsubscribe. Double opt-in (a confirmation email) is the norm and the strongest evidence of consent. It also blocks a child or a third party from signing up someone else's address. Recommended.

### Gaps
- Didn't retrieve current official Cloudflare Web Analytics or Plausible docs on cookies and IP handling, and the sources conflict (see above). Inspect the live site's network and storage in DevTools before claiming "no cookies".
- FTC CAN-SPAM compliance guide not fetched this session (the details above are marked as not re-verified). Source to cite: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- ICO Children's Code text and the ICO's final DUAA cookie guidance were not fetched.
- I found no definitive EU-level statement that cookieless first-party analytics is consent-exempt. Some national DPAs, such as France's CNIL, have audience-measurement exemptions **[not re-verified this session]**.

## Q5. Marketing claims: "educational", "improves math skills", "safe", "COPPA compliant", "no data collected"; FTC on Safe Harbor seals

### Takeaway
FTC Act Section 5 requires claims to be truthful and substantiated before they are made. The FTC has acted against children's "brain training" and educational-benefit claims that lacked scientific backing (Focus Education/Jungle Rangers, WordSmart). It has also acted against false claims of COPPA Safe Harbor membership (Miniclip, 2020). Outcome claims like "improves math skills" carry the highest risk. Descriptive claims such as "practice addition through subtraction" are low risk. "COPPA compliant" is a legal-conclusion claim that the FTC can test. "No data collected" must be literally true for whatever it is attached to.

### Cited Findings
- **Focus Education (Jungle Rangers):** the FTC alleged the game's claims of permanently improving children's "focus, memory, attention, behavior, and school performance" lacked reliable scientific evidence. The order requires future claims to be backed by sufficient evidence and the underlying study data to be preserved. — [Olshan advertising law blog](https://www.olshanlaw.com/Advertising-Law-Blog/ifocus-FTC-Brain-Training-Claims); [ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/the-fine-print/ftc-settles-maker-brain-training-games-children) (commentary; the case is circa 2015)
- **WordSmart:** the FTC charged misrepresentation of educational benefits. An $18.7M judgment was suspended on payment of $147,400. — [Cohen Healthcare Law](https://cohenhealthcarelaw.com/?p=7065) (commentary)
- **CCFC complaints vs Fisher-Price and Open Solutions:** these asked the FTC to limit "educational" to products with clear evidence of benefit. I found no FTC action on them. — [Infopackets](https://infopackets.com/news/8630/baby-app-makers-accused-misleading-parents) (advocacy allegations only)
- **Miniclip (2020):** the FTC alleged Miniclip falsely claimed membership in CARU's COPPA safe harbor program from 2015 to mid-2019 after CARU terminated it. The order prohibits misrepresenting participation or certification in any privacy or security program. Sources conflict on the termination date (2015 per the FTC, July 2016 per one law-firm summary). — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/press-releases/2020/05/swiss-digital-game-developer-settles-ftc-allegations-it-falsely); [Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2020/05/ftc-settles-with-online-game-developer-regarding-allegations-concerning-childrens-privacy/)
- **Safe harbor status:** operators that are members of, and adhere to, an FTC-approved COPPA safe harbor program are deemed in compliance. — [Consumer Financial Services Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2020/05/ftc-settles-with-online-game-developer-regarding-allegations-concerning-childrens-privacy/). FAQ O.1: programs must have an "effective, mandatory mechanism to independently assess member operators' compliance." — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- **FAQ I.14 (verbatim):** "it could be a deceptive practice to misrepresent the level of oversight you provide for a child-directed app." — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- **Apple 2.3.1(a):** misleading marketing "whether within or outside of the App Store" is grounds for removal. This applies to the site's claims too. — [App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **"Educational" / "improves math skills":** prefer feature-descriptive copy ("practice addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with adaptive difficulty", "aligned to [standard] topics" if true). Avoid outcome or efficacy claims ("improves test scores", "raises grades", "proven") unless there is competent and reliable evidence, ideally a study of the app itself. Parent testimonials stating outcomes count as claims the developer must be able to substantiate **[FTC Endorsement Guides, not re-verified this session]**.
- **"Safe":** vague and absolute, so qualify it with specifics: "No ads. No accounts. No chat. No in-app purchases. No data collected by the app."
- **"COPPA compliant":** avoid it as a badge or headline. It invites scrutiny and is a legal conclusion. Better: "Built to collect no personal information from children," which is factual and verifiable. Never display a Safe Harbor seal (CARU, kidSAFE, PRIVO, etc.) unless actually certified and current. Miniclip shows that a lapsed membership claim is itself a violation.
- **"No data collected":** scope it precisely. True for the app if no SDK sends anything (verify crash reporting, Flutter plugins, and any font or CDN calls). False for the website (form, waitlist, analytics, server logs). Write "The Math Dragons app collects no data," not "We collect no data." Keep the App Store "Data Not Collected" label, the Play Data safety form, the privacy policy, and the site copy consistent with each other.

- Didn't fetch FTC statements specifically about the phrase "COPPA compliant" in marketing. The guidance above extrapolates from Miniclip and FAQ I.14.

## Q6. Accessibility: WCAG 2.2 AA, ADA Title III risk, practical checklist

### Takeaway
No US regulation specifies a WCAG version for private (Title III) websites. Courts and plaintiffs use WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA as the de facto benchmark. Litigation volume is high and rising: more than 5,000 digital accessibility suits in 2025 per UsableNet, and 3,117 federal website suits (up 27%) per Seyfarth. About 70% target e-commerce and retail, and repeat defendants are common. A small, non-transactional informational site is lower risk but not immune. Building to WCAG 2.2 AA is cheap at the static-site stage.

### Cited Findings
- **UsableNet 2025 year-end:** more than 5,000 digital accessibility lawsuits across federal and key state courts, 1,427 against prior defendants, and 2,019 in H1 2025. **Seyfarth Shaw:** 3,117 federal website accessibility suits in 2025 vs 2,452 in 2024 (+27%). E-commerce and retail make up about 70% (69-77% in H1 2025). Repeat defendants were 46% of federal cases. — [Newswire summary of UsableNet data](https://www.newswire.com/news/digital-accessibility-lawsuits-surge-in-2025-22618502); [TestParty](https://testparty.ai/blog/ada-lawsuit-trends-ecommerce-2025-2026-data); [Fox Rothschild event page](https://www.foxrothschild.com/events/ada-website-lawsuit-trends-what-2025-filings-mean-for-2026) (secondary or vendor sources; check the original UsableNet and Seyfarth reports)
- **Settlements:** commonly $5,000-$50,000 per one vendor blog citing industry estimates (not a primary dataset). — [accessibilitychecker.org](https://www.accessibilitychecker.org/blog/ada-website-compliance-lawsuits/)

### Inferences
- **Practical WCAG 2.2 AA checklist for this site:**
  - **Text alternatives:** meaningful alt text on dragon art. Decorative art gets `alt=""`.
  - **Video:** captions on all promo videos, and transcripts for origin-story video. Don't autoplay with sound. Provide pause controls for any motion over 5 seconds (2.2.2).
  - **Contrast:** 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 large text and UI components/focus indicators (1.4.3, 1.4.11). Bright kid palettes often fail, so check.
  - **Keyboard:** everything operable by keyboard. Visible focus that isn't obscured by sticky headers (2.4.7, 2.4.11 new in 2.2). No keyboard traps.
  - **Target size:** at least 24x24 CSS px (2.5.8, new in 2.2), which matters for store badges and nav on mobile.
  - **Forms:** visible labels, errors identified in text, no CAPTCHA puzzles without an alternative (3.3.8 Accessible Authentication applies only to auth, but avoid cognitive puzzle CAPTCHAs anyway). Use Cloudflare Turnstile or a honeypot. Don't make users re-enter info (3.3.7 Redundant Entry, new in 2.2).
  - **Structure:** semantic headings, landmarks, `lang` attribute, descriptive link text ("Join the Android beta", not "click here"), and a consistent help/contact location (3.2.6 Consistent Help, new in 2.2).
  - **Motion:** honor `prefers-reduced-motion` for animated dragons and parallax. No flashing more than 3 times per second.
  - **Reflow:** usable at 320 CSS px width and 200% zoom.
  - **Statement:** publish an accessibility statement with a contact for barriers (good-faith signal).
  - **Testing:** automated checks (axe or Lighthouse) plus a manual keyboard pass and one screen-reader pass (VoiceOver or NVDA).
- Avoid accessibility overlay widgets. Plaintiffs' firms have named overlay-using sites in suits **[not re-verified this session]**.

### Gaps
- Didn't fetch the W3C WCAG 2.2 Recommendation this session. My understanding is it was published October 5, 2023, and an ISO version followed **[not re-verified this session]**. Success-criterion numbers above are from prior knowledge.
- **DOJ:** I recall the 2024 Title II rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA for state and local governments, which doesn't directly cover private businesses. I also recall DOJ withdrew its 2022 Title III web guidance in 2025 **[not re-verified this session]**.
- No reliable data was found on lawsuit rates against non-e-commerce informational sites or solo developers specifically.

## Q7. Trademark/IP: App Store, Google Play, and TestFlight badges

### Takeaway
- **Apple:** the App Store badge may be used only for apps *available for download on the App Store*. The only pre-release variant is the pre-order badge. A TestFlight-only beta doesn't qualify for "Download on the App Store", and Apple's marketing guidelines include **no TestFlight badge**.
- **Google:** offers a standard badge and a pre-registration badge, with size and no-modification rules. The page retrieved didn't state a closed-testing variant or the attribution line.
- For the beta phase, use plain text buttons ("Join the iOS beta on TestFlight", "Join the Android beta") instead of store badges. Use Apple's credit-line format wherever Apple trademarks appear.

### Cited Findings
- **Apple Marketing Artwork License, Section 1 (verbatim excerpt):** the Artwork may be used "only in connection with Your applications that are available for download on the App Store ('Applications') and only while You are a member of the Apple Developer Program." — [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- **Apple pre-order (verbatim):** "Use the pre-order badge to indicate that your app is available for pre-order on the App Store. Once the app is released for download, be sure to replace the pre-order badge with the download badge in all of your marketing materials." — [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- **TestFlight:** the Apple marketing guidelines page contains no TestFlight badge and no guidance on mentioning TestFlight. "TestFlight" appears only in site navigation. — [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- **Apple badge rules (verbatim):**
  - "Don't modify, angle, or animate the App Store badge."
  - "Minimum clear space is equal to one-quarter the height of the badge."
  - "Minimum badge height is 10 mm for use in printed materials and 40 px for use onscreen."
  - "Use one App Store badge per layout or video. Place the badge in a subordinate position…"
  - "Never translate App Store or create your own localized badge."
  - "Use only the badge artwork provided in these guidelines. Don't use icons, logos, graphics, or images from www.apple.com to promote your app."
  
  — [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- **Apple trademark attribution (verbatim):**
  - "In communications distributed only in the United States, the appropriate symbol (™, ℠, or ®) must follow each Apple trademark the first time it is mentioned in body copy."
  - Credit lines go once, wherever legal notices appear: "When the App Store badge is used, credit both Apple and the Apple Logo."
  - US format: "_____ and _____ are registered trademarks of Apple Inc."
  - Videos must show credit lines at the end.
  
  — [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- **Apple device imagery (verbatim):** "Use Apple-provided product bezels…", "Use Apple product images 'as is' and without modification", and "Don't show an Apple product along with a competing product from another manufacturer." References to multiple platforms "can be made only in copy or with badges." — [Apple Marketing Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/marketing/guidelines/)
- **Google Play badges (verbatim):**
  - "Google Play badges can be used in marketing campaigns to promote content on Google Play."
  - Pre-registration badge: "Use this version to drive pre-registration on Google Play."
  - Size: "The badge height must be a minimum of 28px" (digital) and "0.3 inches / 7.6mm" (print).
  - Clear space is one-quarter of the badge height.
  - "Don't change the badge color." "Don't remove or rearrange badge elements, or otherwise adjust the badge in any way." "Don't alter the scale of the wordmark or icon." "Don't use outdated badge artwork."
  
  The page didn't include the legal attribution line. — [Google Partner Marketing Hub, Google Play badges](https://partnermarketinghub.withgoogle.com/brands/google-play/google-play/lockups-icons-badges/#badges) (redirected from play.google.com/intl/en_us/badges/)

### Inferences
- **During the beta:** don't use "Download on the App Store". The license limits it to apps available on the App Store, and a TestFlight beta isn't. Pre-order is a different App Store feature. Use a text link: "Join the iOS beta (TestFlight)". Mentioning "TestFlight" in plain text is nominative use. Add "TestFlight" with ™ on first US mention and include it in the Apple credit line (inference; Apple's trademark list wasn't retrieved).
- **Google closed testing:** the opt-in link isn't a public Play listing. The standard "Get it on Google Play" badge implies public availability, and the retrieved page didn't address closed tests. The pre-registration badge applies only if pre-registration is actually set up in Play Console. Use a plain text button for the closed-test opt-in until a public listing or pre-registration exists.
- **At launch:** switch to the official badges, one of each per layout, unmodified. Keep them equal in size relative to each other (common practice), with clear space, and with Apple and Google credit lines in the footer.
- **Product imagery:** use Apple bezels only if showing iPhones or iPads, and don't put an iPhone next to an Android device in the same image. Mixing platforms in imagery is restricted to copy or badges.
