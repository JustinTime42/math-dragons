
**Above the fold (the only thing most people will see)**

- Headline that leads with the problem, not the product. Something like "Math practice your kid will actually ask for" rather than "Math Dragons App"
- One-line subhead that sets up the dragon angle and the dad-built origin
- Screenshot or short demo clip of the app
- The primary CTA: email capture for testing track access. This needs to be google account on their android so it will let them play the closed testing track. 
- "No ads. No account. No in app purchases." — right under the CTA button so it removes objections before they form

**The origin story section** This is your biggest conversion driver. A short version of the story: daughter struggling, searched for apps, everything was broken in the same way, built something myself. Two or three paragraphs. This is what makes a stranger trust you.

**How it works / what's in the app** Brief feature breakdown — which games, which math facts covered (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division), what age range it's built for (5-9). Screenshots of 2–3 actual game screens. Keep this scannable. (I have screenshots here: /dev/math-dragons/math_dragons/assets/images/screenshots)

**Social proof section** Even early, you'll have quotes from testers. Reserve space for this now and populate it as feedback comes in. Even one genuine parent quote is worth more than a paragraph of marketing copy.

**The testing track explainer** A short section that explains what joining actually means: you get early access, you give honest feedback, you get lifetime free access when the full version launches. Make the exchange feel like a fair deal, not a pitch.

**FAQ** Anticipate the obvious questions:

- What devices does it work on? (Android now, iOS coming?)
- What age / grade level? (ages 5-9)
- Is it really free? Will it stay free? (it is 100% free for now. helping test guarantees free forever. possible freemium version in the future)
- What math facts does it cover? (addition, subtraction, multiplication, division of 1-2 digit numbers for now. more coming)
- How is my child's data handled? (zero data stored or accessed. everything stored on device)

**Privacy policy** Yes, absolutely include this — especially because it's an app for kids. COPPA compliance is a real consideration if any data is collected. Even if you collect zero data today, having a clear privacy policy builds trust and you'll need it for app store submissions anyway. Keep it plain-language, not legalese.

**Contact / feedback** A simple form for general feedback separate from the testing track signup. Some people will want to report a bug or ask a question without committing to joining.

**Footer**

- Link to privacy policy
- Contact email
- Social links (LinkedIn, Twitter/X at minimum)
- "Built by a dad in [Alaska]" — this humanizes it and is memorable
